Confidence Modeling: A Reasoning Log

How AuditReady's confidence layer went from a lookup table to a validated record-linkage model — every step, and the reading that justifies it.
dashboardcalibration weight modeldecimal-shift
TL;DR. The inherited "Bayesian confidence" was six hand-set constants — a lookup table keyed by detector type. I rebuilt the near-duplicate channel as the U.S. Census Bureau's record-linkage model (Fellegi–Sunter / Belin–Rubin / BDA3 §1.7), fit unsupervised by EM. It recovered the true duplicates and the per-field evidence weights with no labels. Going deeper revealed the benchmark's errors are deterministic and separable, so honest confidence is near-binary — the "spread" was fiction. To prove the method is nonetheless calibrated, I built a validation harness (simulation-based calibration, BDA3 §6.3): inject fuzzy duplicates at a known rate → ECE = 0.0019, prevalence recovered, with an honest mild underconfidence in the ambiguous zone (the conditional-independence signature). A posterior predictive check on the real data passes 5/6 bins.

3. The model: Fellegi–Sunter record linkage

For each candidate record pair (blocked on part_number) we form an agreement pattern $\gamma=(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_K)$, with $\gamma_k=1$ if the two records match on field $k$. Each pair is a true match $(M)$ or non-match $(U)$ — a latent $z$. Under conditional independence of fields given $z$:

$$P(\gamma_k{=}1\mid M)=m_k,\qquad P(\gamma_k{=}1\mid U)=u_k.$$ $$\mathcal L_M(\gamma)=\prod_k m_k^{\gamma_k}(1-m_k)^{1-\gamma_k},\qquad \mathcal L_U(\gamma)=\prod_k u_k^{\gamma_k}(1-u_k)^{1-\gamma_k}.$$

With prevalence $p=P(M)$, the collection of pairs is a two-component mixture; the posterior responsibility is the calibrated confidence:

$$\boxed{\;P(M\mid\gamma)=\frac{p\,\mathcal L_M(\gamma)}{p\,\mathcal L_M(\gamma)+(1-p)\,\mathcal L_U(\gamma)}\;}$$

We fit $(p,m,u)$ by EM on the unlabeled pairs (E-step = the responsibility above; M-step = $\hat p=\frac1n\sum_i r_i$, $\hat m_k=\frac{\sum_i r_i\gamma_{ik}}{\sum_i r_i}$, $\hat u_k=\frac{\sum_i(1-r_i)\gamma_{ik}}{\sum_i(1-r_i)}$). The field's discriminating power is the log-weight $\log_2(m_k/u_k)$ — bits of evidence.

4. What the model found on the real data

19,013 within-part candidate pairs. EM converged in 5 iterations and, with no labels, recovered exactly the 130 true-duplicate pairs (prevalence $\hat p=0.0068$, expected count $0.0068\times19013=130$). The evidence weights are sharp and interpretable:

But the responsibilities came out binary — 0 or 1, no overlap. Duplicates are exact copies; everything else disagrees on a high-information field. So the honest confidence is ≈1.0 (the hand-set 0.685 was simply miscalibrated, too low), and there is nothing intermediate to calibrate. The same check on orphaned_customer: all 80 orphans are a systematic foreign format (CX-A228, never CU-####), edit-distance 2–3 from any real ID — not typos, a cleanly separate population. Also near-deterministic.

5. The honest finding (which was predicted)

This benchmark's errors are deterministic and separable: exact ×10 decimal shifts, exact-copy duplicates, a distinct ID format. Calibrated confidence is designed for graded ambiguity, and this synthetic data has almost none. The "honest spread 0.55–1.00" we had been proud of was near-binary truth dressed up by hand-set constants. The rigorous methods did not produce a spread — they dissolved a fake one. (This was flagged early in the project as a hypothesis — "the kaggle data itself is synthetic, maybe that's it" — and the analysis confirms it.)

6. Proving calibration when the data can't (BDA3 §6.3)

If the benchmark can't exercise calibration, manufacture ground truth. The harness injects fuzzy near-duplicates at a known rate — clone a record, corrupt 1–6 of 9 fields — so the agreement pattern spans clean → heavily-corrupted. Fit unsupervised, then ask: among pairs scored $P(M){=}q$, are a fraction $\approx q$ truly injected duplicates? Results (800 injected):

The model is well-calibrated at the extremes and mildly underconfident in the ambiguous middle (empirical > predicted). That is the known signature of the conditional-independence assumption — surfaced honestly by the check, not hidden. See the live reliability diagram.

Posterior predictive check on the real data

Draw replicated agreement-score datasets from the fitted model and compare to observed. 5 of 6 score bins fall inside the 95% replicated band — the model reproduces the real structure. The single miss (score 5: observed 3 vs band [0, 2]) is a small, honest signal of mild field-dependence (some coincidental pairs agree on correlated fields slightly more than independence predicts).

7. Decision-theoretic triage (BDA3 §1.7 Fig 1.4 / MacKay Ch 36)

Instead of hand-set 0.60 / 0.90 cutoffs, the operating curve gives false-match rate vs threshold. There is an irreducible ~3.6% false-match floor (coincidental pairs that genuinely agree on most fields), so a 1%-error auto-fix policy honestly requires $P\ge 0.99$. The triage lanes become a decision, derived from a stated error tolerance, not a guess.

8. The weight model, revisited honestly (MacKay Ch 28)

The original decimal-shift error model compared the observed log-weight against a broad Gaussian alternative $\mathcal N(\mu_0,\sigma_{\text{alt}}{=}2.0)$. MacKay's Occam factor explains why that was unsound: a vague alternative's marginal likelihood scales like $1/\sigma_{\text{alt}}$, so the hand-tuned width — not the data — controlled the verdict for borderline cases. The principled replacement models the actual mechanism (an error is the truth shifted by a power of ten), but because shifts are exact ×10ᵏ the two hypotheses are separated by a full decade: every one of the 33 weight findings scores $P=1$. Honest conclusion: the weight channel is near-binary by separation; the mixture is retained for choosing the correction $k$, not for manufacturing a confidence gradient.

9. Honest limitations

  • Conditional independence (Fellegi–Sunter) is mildly violated — the PPC score-5 miss and the ambiguous-zone underconfidence both point to correlated field agreements (dates move together). A log-linear interaction term or a latent-class model with dependence would tighten it.
  • First-moment "calibration" is an identity, not a test. Under EM, $\sum_i r_i = n\hat p$ holds at the optimum, so matching an expected count proves nothing on its own. Real evidence is the reliability diagram and the PPC — which is why those are the artifacts, not a sum.
  • The benchmark is separable, so the calibration claim rests on the injected ambiguity. On genuinely noisy production data the graded zone would be larger and more load-bearing.

10. Open questions (for the modeling lead)

  1. Is conditional independence worth relaxing for these fields, or is the ~3.6% floor acceptable for the use case?
  2. Should orphaned-customer confidence stay near-deterministic (foreign-format ⇒ broken FK) or model a genuine "new customer" hypothesis with a temporal prior?
  3. Is the $P\ge0.99$ auto-fix threshold the right risk posture for a regulated audit, or should the human-review lane be wider?
