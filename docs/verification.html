1. The decimal-shift alternative $H_1$ — correct

The detector flags weights that look like decimal-place errors. The model compares two hypotheses for an observed weight, working in $\log_{10}$ space ($y=\log_{10}\text{weight}$, per-part center $\theta$):

$$H_0:\ y\sim\mathcal N(\theta,\ \sigma_p), \qquad H_1:\ y\sim\sum_{k\in\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm3\}} w_k\,\mathcal N(\theta+k,\ \sigma_p),\quad w_k\propto \rho^{|k|}.$$

The subtlety I most wanted to check: a decimal shift is multiplicative ($\times 10^k$), yet the code adds theta + k. That is only valid in log space — and it is: a $\times 10^k$ error becomes a $+k$ translation of $y$. So each bump $\mathcal N(\theta+k,\sigma_p)$ sits exactly where a $k$-digit shift would land. Coherent.

Minor note carried into the code: error_probability writes $\mathcal N(y;\theta+k)$ while best_decimal_correction writes $\mathcal N(y+k;\theta)$ — opposite sign conventions. It is safe only because the shift set is symmetric with $|k|$-based weights, so $f_1$ is sign-invariant. Flagged in-code in case the prior is ever made asymmetric.

Why this fixes the old, broken $H_1$ — MacKay Ch 28

The previous alternative was a vague $\mathcal N(\mu_0, 2.0)$. MacKay, “Model Comparison and Occam’s Razor,” pp. 343–347, explains exactly why that was self-defeating:

“Simple models tend to make precise predictions. Complex models, by their nature, are capable of making a greater variety of predictions … [so a complex model] must spread its predictive probability $P(D\mid H_2)$ more thinly over the data space.” (p. 345)

A diffuse $H_1$ of width $\sigma_{\text{alt}}$ assigns any plausible $y$ a density $\approx \tfrac{1}{\sigma_{\text{alt}}\sqrt{2\pi}}$ — so the verdict scaled with an arbitrary knob (the Occam factor, $\sim 1/\sigma_{\text{alt}}$). The structured $H_1$ replaces that with a mechanism (decimal shifts), and the verdict is now driven by the likelihood ratio at the data. Verified empirically below.

3. Record linkage — this is the Census Bureau method, verbatim

BDA3 §1.7 “Calibration for record linkage,” pp. 16–18 describes the exact method. The combined score distribution is a two-component mixture (their Eq. 1.7):

$$p(y)=\Pr(\text{match})\,p(y\mid\text{match})+\Pr(\text{non-match})\,p(y\mid\text{non-match}).$$

Fitted from data with unknown match status; a curve of false-match-rate vs. threshold (their Fig. 1.4) lets a decision-maker pick a cutoff. BDA3 even names the failure mode the project first hit:

“Simple methods … lead to extremely inaccurate, typically grossly optimistic, estimates of false-match rates … Records with nominal false-match probabilities of 1% had an actual false-match rate of 5%.” (p. 16)

The Fellegi–Sunter EM in record_linkage.py is the standard latent-class mixture-of-Bernoullis. Verified M-step:

$$\hat p=\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_i r_i,\quad \hat m_k=\frac{\sum_i r_i\gamma_{ik}}{\sum_i r_i},\quad \hat u_k=\frac{\sum_i (1-r_i)\gamma_{ik}}{\sum_i (1-r_i)},\quad r_i=P(M\mid\gamma_i).$$

Cross-check on the “$\sum_i P_i \approx$ matches = calibration” claim: it is not a calibration test. The M-step sets $\hat p=\tfrac1n\sum r_i$, so $\sum_i r_i = n\hat p$ holds by construction at every iteration. Real calibration is the injection harness (ECE = 0.0019) on the calibration page.

Wired into the live pipeline. Near-duplicate confidence was a structural constant (0.82 / 0.58 / 0.52). It is now the Fellegi–Sunter posterior $P(\text{match})$. On the real data: 4,854 records have a candidate partner, $P(\text{match})$ is fully binary (260 at $P\ge0.9$, zero in $(0.05,0.95)$) — confirming the benchmark’s duplicates are deterministic and separable, exactly as the model says.

4. Decision theory for triage — correctly separated from inference

MacKay is explicit (p. 347) that this is a different job:

“Both levels of inference are distinct from decision theory. … Decision theory typically chooses between alternative actions on the basis of these probabilities so as to minimize the expectation of a loss function.”

So the architecture is right: Occam/evidence picks $H_0$ vs $H_1$ (a probability); the triage cutoffs (auto-fix at $P\ge0.99$ for a $\le1\%$ false-match budget) are an expected-loss decision read off the operating curve (MacKay Ch 36 / BDA3 Fig 1.4). Two layers, kept separate.
