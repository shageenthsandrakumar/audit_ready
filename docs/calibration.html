🎯 Calibration Validation

Does the record-linkage model's P(match) mean what it says?
← dashboardweight modeldecimal-shift
The benchmark's real errors are deterministic and separable — they can't test calibration, because there's no graded ambiguity. So we manufacture ground truth: inject fuzzy near-duplicates at a known rate (corrupting 1–6 fields), fit the Fellegi–Sunter model unsupervised, and ask whether P(match)=q actually means "q fraction are true duplicates." This is simulation-based calibration / posterior predictive checking (BDA3 §6.3).

What this proves

The model recovers the injected error rate, recalls the duplicates, and its probabilities track the truth (low ECE). It is well-calibrated at the extremes and mildly underconfident in the ambiguous middle — the known signature of the conditional-independence assumption (Fellegi–Sunter 1969), surfaced honestly by the check rather than hidden. On the clean benchmark, the same model correctly collapses to near-certainty.

Method: Fellegi–Sunter (1969); calibration via Belin–Rubin (1995) / BDA3 §1.7; validation via posterior predictive checking, BDA3 §6.3. Source: calibration_harness.py
